### Where each column came from, and which numbers I had to work out

Equity was already on the Books tab at $100,000.00 for all five books, so
somebody or something filled it in. I checked it rather than trusting it: every
state file in
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/output/` for all
four trading days shows cash of $100,000.00, no positions, no working orders and
realised profit and loss of $0.00. The figure on the sheet is right.

Drawdown I worked out, because it was blank. Each book's scored equity was
$100,000.00 at the open and $100,000.00 at the close on every one of the four
days, with nothing held in between. Peak and trough are the same number, so the
drawdown is 0.00%. One caveat worth saying out loud: the books are only marked at
the daily close, so this is a drawdown measured at four points. That is fine this
week, because a book holding nothing cannot move between those points, but it
will not be fine the week a book actually buys something.

Missed ticks I worked out too, also blank. The ledger's Config tab sets the
cadence at every five minutes from 09:30 to 16:00 New York, which is 79 wake-ups
a day, or 316 across the four trading days. I counted which of those 79 slots
actually produced a tick row per book per day in
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/data/trading.sqlite`.
The day by day result is 75 of 79 on Tuesday, 43 on Wednesday, 46 on Thursday and
77 on Friday. Those match the journals' own counts exactly, which is why I trust
the method. Book A is one ahead of the rest because the 09:30 tick on Tuesday was
killed by the 240 second cap after it had processed book A and before it reached
the other four.

So roughly a quarter of the week's scheduled ticks never happened. Two mornings
account for almost all of it: the loop did not run at all before 12:25 on
Wednesday or before 11:30 on Thursday.

Alpha versus SPY is genuinely not recorded, and I could not work it out. The
SPY Close column on the Daily tab is empty for all four days, so the Books tab's
SPY % and Alpha columns are empty too, and the Summary tab's SPY Return % is
empty. Wednesday's journal explains why in one line: nothing in this project
reads a closing price yet. The journals do carry delayed intraday SPY quotes (for
example a bid of 764.94 at 12:55 on Friday), but a delayed quote from a feed we
already know is unreliable is not a close, and putting one in this column would
be inventing a benchmark rather than measuring one.

Rule triggers needs a health warning. The Books tab formula counts every
non-decision row in the Rules Log, and four of every book's rows are the
end-of-day summary the loop writes at 16:00, one per book per trading day. Those
are not guardrails. Strip them out and the real count of guardrails that fired
this week is zero for books A, B, C and E, and three for book D, which halted on
missing quotes on Monday, Tuesday and Friday. Those halts were the guardrail doing
its job. Note that one of the three landed on Labor Day, when the market was shut,
so book D's headline figure of 7 contains two ordinary trading-day halts and one
holiday.

Commissions and model cost are real zeros rather than blanks. There were no fills
all week, so there was nothing to charge commission on. The model cost is covered
below.

## What the week actually says

Not much about the strategies, and that is the honest headline. Five books over
four days is a small sample in the best case, and this was not the best case.

The comparison the whole experiment exists to make did not run. Books A, B and E run
the same opening momentum strategy on the same shortlist, and the only difference
between them is the model behind the decision: Claude Fable 5.1 on A, no model at
all on B, GPT-6 Astra on E. They finished the week identical in every single
number. That is not a finding about Fable or Astra. It is a finding about dry run.
Friday's journal quotes the tick log directly: in dry run the pick prompt is
rendered and hashed but no model is called, so the answers written down are the
rules-only answers. The database agrees without room for argument. Across 147
decision rows for the week, not one carries a token count, not one carries a
latency, and every cost field that is filled in reads 0.0. Three books named three
different models on their rows and none of the three asked anything.

On top of that, books A, B and E only reached a pick on two of the four days.
Tuesday and Friday they picked from a shortlist holding zero names. Wednesday and
Thursday they never got as far as picking at all, because the loop was not running
before the 10:15 entry cutoff had passed. Only Friday's empty shortlist has a
market reason behind it: four scans returned 50 rows each, 167 names after
duplicates, down through the price, volume and volatility filters to three names,
and none of those three had twice their usual volume in the opening five minutes.
Tuesday's was empty because the scan could not get data.

Books C and D tell us even less. Both are blocked twice over. Book C has never
had a candidate to consider, because the afternoon sweep writes its shortlist file
stamped with the day it ran and the next morning's pick looks for a file named
after the next day. Book D had 15 Congress names in front of it on Tuesday and
Friday and picked nothing both times, because the rules-only path is an opening
range idea with nothing to say about a Congress list, which is exactly what the
loop's own code comment says. So every daily zero from C and D is an artefact of
the plumbing, not a verdict on insider buying or Congress trades.

One week would be nowhere near enough even if all of this were fixed. Four days of
comparable decisions would not settle whether a model beats plain rules on an
opening momentum book. But the point is that we do not have four days of
comparable decisions, we have zero. Nineteen decision packets have been written
since 2026-09-07 across five books and five days and every single one contains
zero candidates. No strategy in this project has yet been tested against a single
real candidate.

Two things on the ledger itself do not match reality, and both are worth fixing
before they mislead somebody. The Daily tab's Notes column says "5 books, 0 missed ticks" on
all four days, which is wrong by about 75 ticks a book. And the Daily tab records
starting and ending equity of $500,000 every day, which is five books times
$100,000 of scored capital, while the broker actually read $1,000,174.27 on
Tuesday and $1,000,690.40 on Friday. The ledger README says the Daily tab tracks
the one real paper account all five books share. It is not doing that. Neither
mistake changed a result this week, because the results are all zero, but both
would corrupt the month-end numbers.

## What broke

Only things that failed more than once are listed. Everything else is in the daily
journals.

### Market data, every single day. Not fixed, and not a bug

The watchdog's market data check failed 75 times on Tuesday, 42 on Wednesday, 45
on Thursday and 77 on Friday. The cause was settled on Wednesday and it is not a
software fault. The paper account is not entitled to real-time prices through the
API, so IBKR answers with errors 10089 and 10168. This is Mo's to buy in Client
Portal. It is also the single reason the scans came back empty on three of the four
days, which makes it the root cause of most of the week's nothing.

### The pre-flight never passed. Four trading days, zero passes

It recorded a verdict of "fail" on Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday, and on Thursday
it left no row at all. The failing checks were market data and reconcile every
time. Since the fix that shipped on Thursday evening, a failed pre-flight writes
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/output/NO_TRADE_TODAY`
and the loop refuses to open anything while that file exists. That is correct
behaviour and it worked. From Thursday onward the books were blocked by their own
safety catch as well as by having nothing to trade. The reconcile half of it will
keep failing every morning until the MCP server stops dying between ticks.

### This Mac going dark, three times in five days. Stopgap only

It slept on battery from about 07:50 to 09:43 on Monday, lost its network for about
ten hours on Wednesday, and stopped running anything at all from about 05:00 to
11:26 on Thursday, again on battery. Monday cost nothing measurable because the
market was shut, but the Wednesday and Thursday mornings account for 69 of the 76
missed ticks on their own. A `caffeinate` process was started by hand at 17:10 on
Thursday, and it does not survive a closed lid or a reboot. The real fix, a watcher
on the Mac Mini that shouts when this MacBook goes quiet, is backlog item 30 and
has not been built. Of everything on this list, this is the one that cost whole
mornings rather than minutes.

### The watchdog restarting a Gateway that was fine, twice

Once on Tuesday from about 12:33 to 12:55, once on Thursday from 13:00 to 13:50.
Both times the network was down and the Gateway was not, so the repair turned a
temporary problem into a shut port for most of an hour. Backlog item 21, still
open. The new fifteen minute restart rule that shipped on Thursday gives the
watchdog a second way to make the same mistake.

### Alerts that never reached Slack, every day

Of 219 alerts raised across the four trading days, 173 reached Slack and 46 did
not: 6 lost on Tuesday, 20 on Wednesday, 19 on Thursday and 1 on Friday. There is
no retry, so a lost alert is lost for good. Backlog item 22, still open. Friday was
nearly clean, which is a network story rather than a fix.

One note for anyone cross-reading the journals. Thursday's entry counts 32 alerts
and 7 Slack losses for that day, while the alerts table holds 87 and 19 for the
same date. The database is the fuller record and it is what this review used.

### Book C's shortlist, all four days

The 16:30 sweep found real names every afternoon: 12 on Tuesday, 8 on Wednesday, 5
on Thursday, 8 on Friday. Every one of them was thrown away unread the next
morning. Backlog item 31, still open.

### Two repeat failures that are now fixed

A Gateway that logs in and then refuses to answer reads broke on three separate
days and the watchdog repaired it unattended each time, which is the watchdog doing
exactly its job. And the 2 AM re-login that IBKR refused on Thursday, costing eight
and a half hours of an open market, was refused again at 03:00 on Friday and
repaired automatically in four minutes by the new `gateway_login` check. A lost day
became a non-event inside twenty-four hours. Nobody knows why IBKR keeps refusing
the re-login, but the workaround holds.

## Cost

The models cost $0.00 this week, and that is a true zero rather than a missing
number. Across 147 decision rows in the database, nothing carries a token count
or a latency, which is what a real call would leave behind. Nineteen rows carry a
cost of exactly 0.0. Every state file reports model spend of 0.0000 dollars. No
call was made because in dry run no call is ever made.

Against that, the five books made and lost $0.00 and paid $0.00 in commission. So
the question this column exists to answer, whether a model earns back what it
costs, has no data behind it at all yet.

The only forecast we have is still the one written on 2026-09-06 in
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/docs/MODELS.md`:
somewhere between $72 and $176 a month across all five books, with a ceiling of
$97 to $234 if every reply runs to its full token budget. That file tells its own
reader to replace the estimate with real numbers after the first week of dry runs.
This was that week, and there are no real numbers to replace it with. The estimate
is now a week older and no better.

One cost detail already measured and still worth carrying: Fable and Astra are
priced identically per token, at $10 per million in and $50 per million out, but
Fable's tokeniser cuts the same prompt into about 30% more pieces (2.84 characters
per token against 4.09). So on identical work Fable will cost meaningfully more
than Astra. That is a real difference in the book A against book E comparison and
it has not been tested once.

The only money actually spent adjacent to this project was $4.50 on 2026-09-06 for
market data, and Tuesday's learning records that it did not buy an API entitlement,
which is the thing that was needed.
