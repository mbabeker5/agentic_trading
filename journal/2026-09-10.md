## What we learned

**The machine and the login are now bigger risks to this project than anything in
the code.** Three trading days, three mornings lost. The causes were a network
outage (Tuesday, 22 minutes), a network outage (Wednesday, ten hours) and today a
rejected password at 02:00 followed by six and a half hours in which this Mac ran
nothing at all. Every piece of code written in the last three days to handle a
broken Gateway has been correct and has not helped, because the Gateway was
rarely the thing that was broken.

**Today distinguished two failure shapes that had looked the same, and the tell
is free.** A Mac with no network still runs its jobs, so alerts still get written
and fail on the Slack leg with a name lookup error. A Mac that is asleep or
stopped writes nothing at all, not even a failed alert. The gap from 05:00 to
11:26 has no alerts of either kind; the window from 12:55 to 13:48 has seven
failed ones. That single difference dates and classifies both outages to the
minute, out of a file the project already writes.

**An account read of exactly 0.00 passes reconciliation.** Confirmed at 12:55:06
today. This is a real hole in the one check that exists to catch the books and
the broker disagreeing, and it is invisible precisely when it matters, because a
book with nothing open matches a broker showing nothing.

**A Gateway that answers on its port is not automatically a Gateway worth
keeping, but restarting one during a network outage makes things worse.** At
13:00 the port was open and the reads were hanging. Both restarts that followed
were correct by their own rules and both made the outage longer, because a
Gateway that cannot reach IBKR cannot finish a login and does not open its port
at all. The check that is missing is not in the watchdog's rules, it is in front
of them: ask whether this Mac has a network before blaming anything else.

**The insider book's afternoon sweep does work that nothing will ever read.**
`shortlist_path()` in `agent/loop.py` line 1209 builds the file name from the day
the sweep runs, and the pick reads the same name for the day it picks. The
insider strategy has two sweeps: 07:00, which its own configuration describes as
"parse everything accepted since last night", and 16:30, described as "parse the
day's filings for tomorrow morning". The 16:30 sweep stamps its output with
today's date, so tomorrow's 09:45 pick will look for tomorrow's file and not find
it. The evidence covers all three trading days: the 07:00 sweep found 0
candidates on 2026-09-08, the only day it has run, and every insider shortlist on
disk was written at about 16:30. Book C has never once had a candidate to
consider. Today's five names, found at 16:32, will be thrown away the same way.

**Both rules shipped this morning work, and both proved their limits the same
day.** The loop brake refused entries on 47 consecutive ticks and sent one alert
per book saying why, all five delivered. The watchdog's fifteen minute rule fired
at 13:20, exactly fifteen minutes after the port went shut at 13:05, once only,
and reported honestly that its restart had not taken. Its real merit today is
negative and still worth having: it stopped the watchdog from staring at a dead
port for hours, which is what happened yesterday.

**Nothing outside this Mac is watching this Mac.** The watchdog exists to notice a
dead loop, and this morning it was dead alongside the loop. The dead man's handle
exists to notice a quiet loop, and it reads the account through the connection
that was down. Every safety net in this project runs on the machine it is meant to
watch, and this morning all of them stopped in the same second and none of them
noticed.

## Proposals for Mo

**1. Fix the Gateway login before 2 AM tonight.** This is the only item here with
a deadline. At 02:00 this morning IBC clicked "Re-login" and IBKR answered
"Unrecognized Username or Password", and the same script will run again at 02:00
tomorrow with the same credentials. The password lives in plain text in
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/output/gatewaystart.filled.sh`,
so the first check is whether it still matches the account, and the second is
whether IBKR has forced a password change or is now requiring a mobile approval
that an automated re-login cannot give. Cost: ten minutes and a log in to Client
Portal. Risk: none, and the alternative is losing tomorrow morning the same way.

**2. Put a watcher on the Mac Mini that shouts when this MacBook goes quiet.**
Three mornings lost and in all three cases nothing told anybody until a person
looked. Every alarm this project has runs on the machine it is watching, so when
the machine stops, so do the alarms. The Mini at 10.0.0.175 is already set up,
already reachable over SSH, and is not a laptop that sleeps. The smallest version
is one cron job on the Mini that reads
`/Users/mtalib/workspace_repos/personal_repo/agentic_trading/output/heartbeat`
over SSH every ten minutes and sends one Slack message when it stops moving
during market hours. Cost: an hour of work and a handful of lines. Risk: near
zero, it only reads a file, and it is the only proposal here that would have
caught all three lost mornings.

**3. Find out why this Mac keeps stopping.** Two different faults hide under one
symptom: the network keeps going away (three times in three days) and today the
whole machine appears to have gone away for six and a half hours. The first thing
to run is `pmset -g log | grep -E "Sleep|Wake"` on this Mac, which takes seconds
and will say straight away whether today was sleep. If it was, the fix is a power
setting and a promise not to shut the lid, and it is free. If it was not, we have
a worse problem and need to know tonight. This was proposal 1 yesterday and it did
not happen. Cost: minutes. Risk: none.

**4. Ask whether the Mac has a network before restarting anything.** One check in
front of the watchdog's restart rules: resolve IBKR's host name, or fetch a known
page, with a short deadline. If that fails, alert "the Mac has no network,
leaving the Gateway alone" and skip the restart. This is backlog item 21, already
open, and today it cost 49 minutes of a shut port that the watchdog itself
created. Cost: a small function and one test that fakes the name lookup failing.
Risk: on a day when IBKR itself is unreachable but the rest of the internet is
fine, the watchdog would sit still, which is the safe direction.

**5. Treat an account worth exactly 0.00 as a failed read, not as a fact.** One
condition in the reconciliation path: if the broker reports zero equity on an
account that is funded, that is "the broker could not be read", the branch that
already exists and already halts nobody, rather than a number the books get
compared against. Cost: a few lines and one test. Risk: a genuinely empty account
would be reported as unreadable, which is the safe direction to be wrong in, and
does not apply to a paper account holding a million dollars.

**6. Make the afternoon insider sweep write tomorrow's file.** The 16:30 sweep
says in its own configuration that it is parsing "the day's filings for tomorrow
morning", then stamps its output with today's date, where no pick will ever look.
Either the sweep writes `insider_shortlist_<tomorrow>.json`, or the 09:45 pick
falls back to the most recent shortlist within a day or two. The first is smaller
and matches the stated intent. Cost: one line plus a test. Risk: a shortlist built
before the close is a few hours stale by the next morning's pick, which is the
design the strategy file already describes. Without this, book C cannot ever pick
anything, and we would keep reading its daily zero as a market result rather than
a plumbing fault.

**7. Move the two morning sweeps out from behind the Gateway check.** Backlog item
24, open since yesterday, and it has now cost book D two full days and book C one.
Neither sweep talks to IB Gateway at all: one reads SEC EDGAR and the other reads
House and Senate disclosures. They sit behind a port check in `run_tick.sh` that
exits the whole tick before they run. Cost: small, either move the sweep phase
