## Hub decision: two books may now hold the same ticker (2026-09-06, evening)

**Mo can overturn this. It was decided by the hub, not by him, and it changes a
rule the review team had called a blocking finding the same morning.**

What the rule was. One ticker, one book, at any moment. No two books could hold,
or have a working entry order in, the same name. The reasoning was that IBKR
nets positions per symbol inside the one shared paper account, so if book A held
100 AAPL and book B bought 100 more, the broker would report one line of 200
shares and there would be no way left to say whose was whose.

Why it is being dropped. The netting is real, but exclusivity was never what
solved it. Attribution needs two things and both already exist: every order
carries the book's own order reference tag, and every book keeps its own record
of what it holds. Given those, two books in the same name is an arithmetic
problem, not an unanswerable one.

What it was costing. Books A, B and E run the SAME strategy on the same
shortlist. That is the whole comparison month one exists to make. Under
exclusivity, whichever book ticked first took the top name and the other two were
locked out of it, so the three books were never actually choosing from the same
menu. Two independent strategies picking the same name on the same morning is
the agreement we are trying to measure, and the rule was throwing that signal
away and calling it safety.

What replaces it. Reconciliation stops comparing per book and compares per
symbol: for every ticker, the broker's net position has to equal the sum of what
every book believes it holds. When they disagree, every book holding that symbol
is halted, and a book holding nothing in that name carries on untouched.

It also catches more than the old check did. A disagreement about a name two
books share is something exclusivity prevented anyone from ever seeing, because
the second book was never allowed in.

The second layer underneath it. Momentum v2's `account_symbol_cap` (item A9,
approved by Mo the same day) caps any one ticker at 15 percent of what all five
books are worth, counted across every book. That was written as a backstop under
exclusivity. With exclusivity gone it is doing real work, and it is now the thing
that stops the five books piling into one name.

If Mo disagrees, the change is small to undo: the rule id `symbol_exclusive` is
still there and still emits, it just reports rather than refuses.
